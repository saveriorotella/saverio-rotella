I am an ESRC-funded PhD Candidate in Political Science at the [Centre for Institutions and Political Behaviour (CIPB)](https://www.durham.ac.uk/research/institutes-and-centres/institutions-political-behaviour/) at Durham University. 

My research sits at the crossroads of Political Economy and Comparative Politics. My thesis, "The Long Hand of Inequality: How Pre-Transition Economic Disparities Shape Contemporary Democratic Backsliding", examines theoretically and empirically how pre-transition income inequality generates structural vulnerability to erosion in Third Wave democracies. I argue that economic disparities lead to democratic erosion because they incentivise elites to design weak formal institutions and they concentrate informal economic powers. My work involves advanced quantitative methods, including multi-mediation analysis, staggered difference-in-differences, and survey data.

Before joining Durham University, I graduated cum laude (8.9/10) from the Research Master's in International Relations at the [University of Groningen](https://www.rug.nl/). I received a BA in Political Science and International Relation at the [University of Perugia](https://www.unipg.it/en/) with a 110/110 cum laude. I also conducted two EU-funded study exchanges (Erasmus+) at [KU Leuven](https://www.kuleuven.be/english/kuleuven) and the [University of Antwerp](https://www.uantwerpen.be/en/), and a EU-funded traineeship at the University of Groningen.

My research and commentary have appeared in various Italian and European think tanks and outlets — you can find my work [here](https://saveriorotella.github.io/year-archive/).
